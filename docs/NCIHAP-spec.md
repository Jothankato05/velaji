One Child. One Health Record. Every Vaccine. Everywhere.

## 1. EXECUTIVE CONCEPT

The National Child Immunisation & Health Assurance Programme (NCIHAP) is proposed as a Federal Government digital public-health infrastructure that creates a lifelong, verifiable immunisation record for every Nigerian child from birth.

At birth or first contact with the health system, every child is registered and assigned a Child Health Identification Number (CHIN) linked, where available and legally permissible, to the child's National Identification Number, birth-registration record and parent/guardian identity.

The child's vaccination history is then maintained in a secure national registry accessible through:

- a physical smart health card;
- QR verification;
- mobile application;
- USSD;
- SMS;
- authorised healthcare terminals;
- secure web portal; and
- approved integration with national health information systems.

The system does not simply record vaccines.

It knows:

Who has been vaccinated.
Who is due.
Who is overdue.
Who has missed a dose.
Where the child is.
Which facility administered the vaccine.
What intervention is required next.

The programme then introduces a powerful behavioural and health-financing component:

«Complete the nationally prescribed childhood immunisation schedule and unlock a defined period of NHIA-supported health coverage for the child, subject to programme eligibility and government approval.»

This transforms immunisation from an isolated clinical activity into the child's gateway into Nigeria's organised healthcare system.

## 2. THE NATIONAL VISION

Nigeria should move from:

“Did this child receive a vaccine?”

to:

“Where is this child on his or her complete health journey?”

The programme creates a national digital chain:

BIRTH → IDENTITY → IMMUNISATION → FOLLOW-UP → COMPLETION → HEALTH COVERAGE → CONTINUED INSURANCE

Every Nigerian child should become visible to the health system from birth.

## 3. THE PROBLEM BEING SOLVED

Nigeria faces two different challenges:

ZERO-DOSE CHILDREN

Children who never enter the routine immunisation system.

DROP-OUT CHILDREN

Children who begin vaccination but fail to complete subsequent scheduled doses.

The second group requires more than awareness campaigns.

It requires continuity infrastructure.

Paper vaccination cards can be lost.

Parents relocate.

Phone numbers change.

Families travel.

Children attend different hospitals.

Facilities may not share records.

Caregivers forget appointments.

Health authorities often discover coverage gaps retrospectively.

NCIHAP converts this fragmented system into a child-centred national record that follows the child rather than the health facility.

## 4. THE CHILD HEALTH IDENTIFICATION NUMBER — CHIN

Every registered child receives a unique Child Health Identification Number (CHIN).

Example:

CHIN: NG-25-09-18472639

The CHIN becomes the child's healthcare reference for immunisation until full interoperability with the national identity ecosystem is achieved.

It should be linked, subject to appropriate laws and approvals, with:

- National Identification Number;
- NPC birth-registration record;
- parent/guardian NIN;
- state of registration;
- LGA;
- PHC/facility;
- NHIA enrolment;
- immunisation history; and
- other approved child-health records.

CRITICAL PRINCIPLE

The CHIN identifies the health record. The NIN identifies the person.

The programme should therefore complement Nigeria's existing national identity infrastructure rather than duplicate it.

## 5. NATIONAL CHILD HEALTH SMART CARD

Every registered child receives a durable National Child Health Card.

The card could contain:

Front

- Child's name
- photograph, when appropriate
- CHIN
- QR code
- date of birth
- basic identification information

Back

- emergency information
- verification instructions
- authorised programme contacts

The card should function similarly to a banking card in simplicity — but it should not store sensitive medical information openly.

The QR code/card identifier retrieves authorised information from the secure national registry.

A lost card therefore does not mean a lost vaccination history.

A replacement card can simply be issued.

## 6. HOW THE SYSTEM WORKS

STAGE 1 — BIRTH

A child is born at a hospital, PHC, maternity centre or other recognised facility.

The birth is registered.

The child's digital health profile is created.

Where technically and legally possible, the platform interfaces with NPC/NIMC systems rather than creating duplicate identity processes.

STAGE 2 — FIRST IMMUNISATION

The vaccine administered is recorded digitally.

The system records:

- vaccine;
- dose;
- date;
- facility;
- administering health worker;
- batch/lot information where required;
- next vaccine;
- next appointment date; and
- vaccination status.

The next appointment is calculated automatically from the approved national schedule.

## 7. THE REMINDER ENGINE

The platform should communicate before a vaccine becomes overdue.

Example workflow:

7 DAYS BEFORE

SMS/app notification.

«“Your child's next immunisation is due in 7 days.”»

3 DAYS BEFORE

Second reminder.

24 HOURS BEFORE

Final appointment reminder.

DUE DATE

Notification with facility/location information.

3 DAYS OVERDUE

Caregiver receives an overdue alert.

7 DAYS OVERDUE

Secondary caregiver is contacted where consent and programme rules permit.

CONTINUED DEFAULT

The child enters an authorised follow-up queue for PHC/community-health intervention.

The objective is simple:

«The system should identify the missed child before the missed child disappears from the health system.»

## 8. MULTI-CHANNEL ACCESS

Nigeria cannot build this programme assuming everyone owns a smartphone.

Therefore NCIHAP should operate simultaneously through:

- Smartphone application
- SMS
- USSD
- Interactive Voice Response
- Call centre
- Web portal
- PHC terminals
- Offline-capable healthcare application
- Community health-worker devices

A mother with a basic phone should receive essentially the same critical reminder as a parent using an iPhone.

## 9. OFFLINE-FIRST TECHNOLOGY

This is essential.

A health worker in a location without internet connectivity should still be able to:

1. search locally available patient information;
2. record vaccination;
3. issue/update proof of vaccination;
4. schedule the next dose; and
5. synchronise the transaction when connectivity returns.

The programme must therefore be designed for Nigeria's actual connectivity environment, not an ideal connectivity environment.

## 10. NATIONAL IMMUNISATION STATUS

Each child's dashboard can use simple operational statuses:

GREEN — ON TRACK

Vaccination schedule current.

AMBER — DUE SOON

Upcoming vaccination.

RED — OVERDUE

Scheduled vaccination missed.

GREY — UNVERIFIED/INCOMPLETE RECORD

Requires reconciliation.

BLUE — SCHEDULE COMPLETED

Age-appropriate programme completed.

This gives parents, health workers and authorised programme administrators an immediate picture of status.

## 11. NATIONAL COMMAND DASHBOARD

The greatest government value is not the card.

It is the intelligence created behind the card.

Authorised decision-makers could see aggregated, privacy-protected information such as:

Nigeria → State → LGA → Ward → PHC

and determine:

- children registered;
- doses administered;
- children due this week;
- overdue children;
- zero-dose estimates/case-finding;
- completion rates;
- vaccine utilisation;
- geographic dropout clusters;
- facilities with unusual dropout rates;
- outreach requirements;
- stock pressure;
- performance trends.

Nigeria would move toward real-time intervention rather than retrospective reporting.

## 12. IMMUNISATION COMPLETION INCENTIVE

This is one of the strongest components of the proposal.

Upon verified completion of the nationally defined childhood immunisation milestone, the child becomes eligible for an NHIA Child Health Reward, subject to the funding model and NHIA rules approved for the programme.

For example:

COMPLETE IMMUNISATION → 12 MONTHS SPONSORED CHILD HEALTH COVERAGE

This should not be presented as “payment for vaccination.”

It should be positioned as:

Healthy Start Health Coverage

The government rewards preventive-health participation by extending healthcare protection beyond vaccination.

## 13. WHY THE NHIA COMPONENT IS STRATEGIC

The incentive solves more than one problem.

It can:

Increase immunisation completion.

Introduce families to health insurance.

Increase awareness of NHIA and State Social Health Insurance Schemes.

Create first-time insurance users.

Reduce dependence on out-of-pocket healthcare expenditure.

Create an insurance-renewal pipeline.

Strengthen preventive healthcare behaviour.

After the sponsored period, parents receive renewal messages:

«“Your child's Healthy Start coverage expires in 30 days. Continue protecting your child by renewing health insurance.”»

Government has therefore used immunisation to introduce an entire household to health insurance.

## 14. A BETTER INCENTIVE MODEL

Rather than waiting until the very last vaccine, incentives can be structured in stages.

MILESTONE 1

Birth registration + initial immunisation.

Digital certificate/badge.

MILESTONE 2

Major schedule checkpoint completed.

Health/wellness benefit.

MILESTONE 3

Age-appropriate immunisation schedule completed.

Healthy Start NHIA coverage activated.

This maintains engagement throughout the vaccination journey.

## 15. INCENTIVES SHOULD NEVER BECOME PUNISHMENT

A critical policy safeguard:

A child who misses vaccination should never be denied essential healthcare, education or other fundamental services merely because of incomplete vaccination records.

The platform should encourage, remind, locate and support.

It should not punish children for circumstances beyond their control.

## 16. VERIFICATION ANYWHERE IN NIGERIA

An authorised healthcare worker scans the child's QR code or enters the CHIN.

The system could return:

IMMUNISATION STATUS: CURRENT

or

IMMUNISATION ATTENTION REQUIRED

with only the information that person is authorised to access.

A Lagos-registered child visiting Kano could therefore continue the vaccination schedule without starting another paper record.

The record follows the child nationally.

## 17. FRAUD PREVENTION

A national incentive programme will attract fraudulent claims unless controls are designed from the beginning.

Every vaccination transaction should therefore have appropriate verification mechanisms such as:

- registered facility identity;
- authorised healthcare-worker credentials;
- timestamp;
- vaccine/dose validation;
- batch number where applicable;
- duplicate-dose detection;
- abnormal activity detection;
- audit trail;
- facility-level reconciliation; and
- periodic verification.

Artificial intelligence and statistical monitoring could flag unusual patterns such as one provider supposedly vaccinating an impossible number of children within a short period.

## 18. VACCINE SUPPLY INTELLIGENCE

The platform can eventually connect vaccination demand with vaccine logistics.

Government can see:

10,420 children will require Vaccine X in LGA Y during the next four weeks.

That creates the possibility of predictive stock planning.

The system can help forecast:

- vaccine demand;
- stock requirements;
- cold-chain requirements;
- geographic deployment;
- staffing needs;
- outreach requirements.

NCIHAP therefore evolves from a vaccination database into public-health planning infrastructure.

## 19. CHILDREN BORN OUTSIDE FORMAL HEALTH FACILITIES

The programme must not exclude home births.

Registration channels should include:

- PHCs;
- approved hospitals;
- community health workers;
- mobile registration teams;
- NPC registration points;
- approved outreach programmes.

Once identified, the child enters the same national health pathway.

## 20. NOMADIC, DISPLACED AND MOBILE POPULATIONS

The system is particularly valuable for:

- internally displaced families;
- nomadic communities;
- migrant families;
- riverine communities;
- border communities;
- families relocating between states.

Because the health record is national rather than facility-based, continuity can survive relocation.

## 21. CHILD HEALTH WALLET

The concept can ultimately grow beyond immunisation.

The same secure child-health account could eventually contain authorised records relating to:

- immunisation;
- growth monitoring;
- Vitamin A;
- nutrition;
- malaria interventions;
- sickle-cell screening;
- newborn screening;
- developmental milestones;
- referrals;
- NHIA status;
- selected laboratory information;
- school-health information.

This should happen progressively rather than attempting everything in Version 1.

## 22. GOVERNMENT INSTITUTIONS

The project should be government-led.

Federal Ministry of Health and Social Welfare

Policy leadership and programme ownership.

NPHCDA

Routine immunisation leadership and implementation standards.

NHIA

Health-insurance incentive design and coverage.

National Population Commission

Birth-registration interoperability.

NIMC

Identity interoperability.

State Ministries of Health / State PHC Development Agencies

State Health Insurance Agencies

Insurance integration where applicable.

Federal Ministry of Communications, Innovation & Digital Economy

Digital-public-infrastructure alignment.

NCC / Telecom Operators

## 23. DEVELOPMENT PARTNERS

Subject to government engagement, the programme could seek technical or financing partnerships from organisations working in immunisation, child health and digital health, including:

- WHO;
- UNICEF;
- Gavi;
- World Bank;
- development finance institutions;
- philanthropic health organisations;
- technology partners;
- Nigerian telecommunications operators.

Government must nevertheless retain sovereignty over the national health infrastructure and citizen data.

## 24. PRIVACY BY DESIGN

A national child-health database requires exceptional safeguards.

The architecture should incorporate:

- encryption at rest and in transit;
- role-based access;
- least-privilege permissions;
- parent/guardian consent mechanisms where applicable;
- immutable audit trails;
- breach-response procedures;
- data minimisation;
- secure APIs;
- identity verification;
- disaster recovery;
- geographically resilient infrastructure;
- defined data-retention policies;
- compliance with Nigerian data-protection legislation.

A QR scan should never expose a child's entire medical history to anyone holding a smartphone.

Access must depend on authorisation.

## 25. IMPLEMENTATION ROADMAP

PHASE I — POLICY & DESIGN | 0–6 MONTHS

Establish Presidential/Ministerial steering structure.

Complete:

- policy framework;
- institutional responsibilities;
- legal assessment;
- data-protection impact assessment;
- funding model;
- NHIA incentive structure;
- technical architecture;
- identity architecture;
- immunisation workflow;
- interoperability standards;
- programme KPIs.

PHASE II — TECHNOLOGY & PILOT | 6–12 MONTHS

Build Minimum Viable National Platform.

Pilot across deliberately different environments, for example:

- FCT — urban/peri-urban;
- Lagos — high-volume metropolitan;
- Kano — high birth cohort;
- Nasarawa — mixed urban/rural;
- one hard-to-reach/conflict-affected setting subject to government security assessment.

Measure:

- registration;
- reminder delivery;
- vaccination completion;
- dropout reduction;
- offline performance;
- health-worker workload;
- fraud;
- caregiver satisfaction;
- cost per child.

PHASE III — REGIONAL EXPANSION | 12–24 MONTHS

Expand based on evidence.

Train state administrators, PHCs and community health workers.

Integrate progressively with approved government systems.

PHASE IV — NATIONAL ROLLOUT | 24–36 MONTHS

Deploy across:

36 States + FCT → 774 LGAs → Wards → PHCs

National communications campaign begins.

PHASE V — CHILD HEALTH ECOSYSTEM

Expand carefully from immunisation into broader maternal and child health services.

## 26. NATIONAL PERFORMANCE INDICATORS

The programme should be judged by outcomes, not app downloads.

Primary KPIs:

Zero-dose reduction

Dose-to-dose dropout reduction

Full immunisation completion rate

On-time vaccination rate

Percentage of births linked to digital child-health records

Percentage of PHCs reporting digitally

Overdue children successfully recovered

Average time between missed appointment and intervention

NHIA incentive activation

Insurance continuation/renewal after sponsored coverage

Cost per additional fully immunised child

## 27. FINANCING POSSIBILITIES

A blended financing structure could include:

- Federal Government appropriation;
- Basic Health Care Provision Fund where legally applicable;
- NHIA-approved financing mechanisms;
- State counterpart funding;
- development-partner grants;
- Gavi-supported immunisation investments where eligible;
- World Bank health-system programmes;
- private-sector CSR;
- telecommunications partnerships;
- outcome-based financing.

The programme should ultimately have a government-owned sustainability model, rather than depend indefinitely on donors.

## 28. THE NATIONAL VALUE PROPOSITION

NCIHAP simultaneously addresses:

IMMUNISATION

More children complete vaccination.

IDENTITY

Children enter formal government systems earlier.

HEALTH INSURANCE

Families are introduced to organised health coverage.

DATA

Government gains better decision-making intelligence.

PRIMARY HEALTHCARE

PHCs gain continuity and follow-up tools.

PUBLIC FINANCE

Resources can increasingly be targeted toward actual gaps.

UNIVERSAL HEALTH COVERAGE

Children become an entry point for household participation in organised healthcare.

## 29. WHAT MAKES THE PROJECT NATIONAL

This must not be pitched merely as another mobile application.

Apps come and go.

This should be pitched as:

«Nigeria's National Digital Infrastructure for Childhood Immunisation Continuity and Early-Life Health Assurance.»

The smartphone application is only one interface.

The actual national asset is the interoperable registry, identification architecture, reminder and recovery engine, health-worker network, analytics platform and insurance pathway behind it.

## 30. THE POLICY PROPOSITION

The proposition to Government is therefore simple:

«Register every child.
Give every child a health identity.
Record every vaccine.
Predict every next dose.
Follow every missed child.
Reward completion with health protection.
Connect families to health insurance.
Give Government real-time visibility.»

Nigeria should reach a point where a child's vaccination history does not disappear because a paper card disappeared.

A child should not become invisible because the family relocated.

And a missed vaccination appointment should not remain unnoticed for months.

## 31. THE BIGGER VISION

Today:

National Child Immunisation & Health Assurance Programme.

Tomorrow:

Nigeria's lifelong digital preventive-health infrastructure.

The child registered today could eventually move through:

Birth → Immunisation → Child Health → School Health → Adolescent Health → Adult Health Insurance

under interoperable national health infrastructure.

That is how the project becomes bigger than vaccination.

It becomes a foundation for Universal Health Coverage and preventive healthcare in Nigeria.
